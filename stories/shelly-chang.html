Shelly Chang

Learning the habits of a researcher

Shelly Chang’s interest in biology grew through teachers and mentors who made experimental science engaging. At Academia Sinica, her doctoral research involved antibodies and their relevance to therapeutic development.

The work

She worked with Whitaker fellow Teagan Adamson in 2013 at the Institute of Biomedical Sciences. In her interview, she described learning not only experimental techniques but also how to organize a research question and plan the steps needed to investigate it.

The perspective

Mentorship remained central to her story. Her advisor helped her develop a more logical approach to research and greater confidence using English in a scientific setting. These everyday forms of guidance were part of becoming an independent scientist.
