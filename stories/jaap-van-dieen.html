Jaap van Dieën

Asking how people keep their balance

Jaap van Dieën’s biomechanics research investigated everyday actions that become complex when examined closely: maintaining posture, walking, and keeping balance. His work connected those questions with aging, neurological conditions, and low-back pain.

The work

At VU Amsterdam, he described research on trunk control and walking, including collaborations involving predictive foot placement and exoskeletons. Understanding movement required looking at both mechanical demands and the ways people respond to them.

The perspective

His interview also reflected enjoyment of the variety in academic work, from teaching to developing research with students. The connection between movement in daily life and movement as a scientific problem gave the work a broad, human context.
