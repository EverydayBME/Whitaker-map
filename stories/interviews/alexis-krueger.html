Background and career path

I had a phenomenal high school math teacher that really set the stage for me to do well. He had PhD's and had formed and sold a technology startup, then started teaching high school math because he liked working with students. He was an incredibly good teacher who challenged us, had real world examples, and made math bigger than just the equations. For example, our final was designed to be half about the math and half about the realities of teamwork as an employee, including group think. He talked a lot about careers and college, introducing us to and encouraging us to explore different options. A challenge for me was that biomedical engineering tends to be concentrated in a few hotspots around the nation. And as it happens, those hotspots are not where my family is. As a new grad I started out with an amazing biomedical engineering job that I absolutely loved in one of those hotspots, but I hated living there, far from my family. A few years into it I started to consider moving nearer to my family, but this was a very challenging idea to me because I felt like it was "giving up" or "failing". Taking a less-ideal job that had a more ideal location near my family is not what young folks are "supposed to do". I had defined what success meant to me personally some years ago (a combination of family and fulfillment), but I struggled to commit to it because it ran counter to the working culture I was around. I am happy to say now that I own a 5 acre hobby farm down the road from my family. Its outside a college town where I'm working at an OK engineering job while transitioning into teaching engineering, which was a passion of mine I discovered during my Whitaker grant. I am happily aligning my life to what's most important to me. I get to do engineering that I love in a way that I love, that complements, rather than detracts, from my life outside of work. This is success in science for me. I just had to let go of my perception of other's definition of success in order to get there.

The international experience

My Whitaker fellowship was an experience extremely different than anything I had been exposed to before, and that really helped me to narrow in on what is important to ME in my career and life. Seeing people live and work quite differently than what I was used to opened my perspective and got me thinking more creatively about my career. When I had seen a wider range of living and working I was able to pick out the things that truly mattered to me - many of which are not what I would have expected prior to leaving the country. I did my Whitaker fellowship as a Master's candidate. As part of the Master's degree, I worked with undergrad students, from both my home and host countries. I absolutely loved it! I loved teaching and listening with my students. My mentor at my host institution was phenomenal and we still stay connected today discussing careers in teaching and dreams. I am transitioning from industry to academia to be a teacher and am quite excited about this. I had always had a latent interest in teaching and teaching across cultural barriers, but without my Whitaker experience I think this would have stayed dormant, as a back of the mind "maybe one day". Now I realize this is something I can do, I want to do, and am doing.

Work and career reflections

There are way more career pathways then I thought as a high school student! I thought my engineering options were "do a mechanical engineering degree and be a mechanical engineer, do a computer science degree and be a programmer, do an electrical engineering degree and be an electrical engineer". That is not true at all. There are many many different options within each of those, many other options between them, and many opportunities to move around between them. What you study in college does not tie you into a particular career path by any means, nor does each degree correspond to one career path. Your degree is a jumping off point, and only that. My younger self felt that I was making some kind of life-changing and permanent step when I choose a major. I would remove that stress, that feeling of pressure, and let my younger self know that its not permanent, that I am not setting my life on an unchangeable path when I choose a major.
