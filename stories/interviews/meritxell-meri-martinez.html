Thank you for making time on a Friday afternoon to chat with us from San Antonio! May we start with a brief introduction about you?

My full name is Meritxell Martinez, but I go by Meri. I graduated with my bachelor degree in Biomedical Engineering from the University of Texas at San Antonio just this past May, so I guess you could call me a Coronavirus graduate.

I am currently an intern at a medical device startup company, where I joined as a product development engineer last March. It is funny because it is a very small startup. By small, I mean that I am employee number one, and that’s it. I work closely with the CTO and there is a CEO as well, but everyone else are contract-based consultants. It has been really fun, not only to work as an engineer there, but also to work in this environment where I see a lot of aspects of engineering – like what it takes to start a medical device company, to see our work come together, and to make something out of it.

As a product development engineer, I mostly focus on design. Have you heard of “Negative Pressure Wound Therapy?” It first started in the ‘90s by a large company nearby called KCI, which was actually just bought by 3M. Negative pressure wound therapy is the application of negative pressure, like a vacuum, to chronic wounds like foot ulcers and bed sores. KCI was a pioneer in negative pressure wound therapy. Our founders used to work for KCI, where they developed a project using negative pressure for orthopedic injuries. They decided to license that technology and created a new company out of it. So that is what we are doing – negative pressure therapy for orthopedic wounds. They have already completed animal studies in sheep and rabbits, and they found that negative pressure applied to injured bones promotes better and faster healing.

The work is very interesting, but my role in the company has been more on the technical side. I have been working on testing this device, because a medical device has to go through different phases from conception to market introduction. I first came in during the design phase, but we are currently in the verification and validation phase, which involves a lot of testing to verify that the device performs as intended. We also perform validations with surgeons and health care professionals so that they can determine if the device functions according to what they think it should do. It has been a lot of fun and a great learning experience. We are currently putting together a 510(k) application, which is what you need to get the device cleared by the FDA. So, we hope to have this ready soon.

That sounds not only exciting, but very important. How has your work been affected by the COVID-19 pandemic?

Well, when everything first started, we were out of work for about 3 weeks. After that, we returned to the office quite easily since it is just me and my boss. We could maintain social distance without concern for too many people being in the same room.

[The pandemic] definitely affected the timeline we had in mind. Originally, I was supposed to start my full-time engineering position sometime in the summer, but it got pushed back. As a startup you really rely on investors, but right now investors do not want to hand over any money. Not only does this push back the timeline, but also it generates a feeling of uncertainty. So far, I have been able to stay on as an intern. Hopefully, I will be able to obtain a full-time position next year. If not, I will apply for other jobs and just go with the flow.

With your technical expertise and experience, we are sure you will be successful. Can you share with us a little bit about your journey into the field of biomedical engineering?

Yes! It is a good question. When I graduated high school, I was thinking about what I should study. In high school we always took these tests that told you what you were good at. I always got the same two things – science and art – which initially surprised me because I did not see how they were related and felt like it was hard to focus on one versus the other. I was leaning more towards science. Both of my parents are doctors in Mexico, so I always had an underlying pressure of becoming a doctor, but I never really wanted to for some reason. I always really enjoyed math, so when I started college, I considered being a math major. It was in my first pre-calculus class that I decided I did not want to only do math. Math was fun, but I wanted to do something more with it. At the same time, I was definitely listening to my friends around me who were going into engineering.

In the beginning, I just was not really sure what engineering was. I always pictured somone fixing a car and didn’t really see myself doing that. Once in college and I listened to my engineering friends talk about their classes where they designed things to solve problems, I thought, “That sounds really interesting.”

Then, I heard about the biomedical engineering field from another friend and ultimately decided to go into it. I guess I went into BME blind to be completely honest. I didn’t know what to expect and even being in the BME program for the first year I think I was still very confused as to what BME really was because it is such an interdisciplinary field. You can do so many different things. You could talk to a biomedical engineer that is doing research and another that is in the medical device industry and what they do is so different.

Besides interning, you cofounded The BME Life YouTube channel. Can you tell us more about it?

Sure! As I was saying, in college I found myself kind of confused. I would try to look online, searching like “What is biomedical engineering?” You can find some information online, but I really wanted to see someone who was doing it, someone that I could look up to and think “Maybe I can do this!” I always feel like you can learn about everything in books, but when you listen to someone who has gone through it then it is a whole different experience. So it started with, “Let’s try this out and see if we can put some biomedical engineering facts out there.” I think our first video is an excel tutorial – it began very random. Then I started doing podcasts and I really think that is what I like the most about the channel. We really enjoy doing podcasts because you invite other people who are in the field or a related field to share their experience, and I always think that’s very valuable because someone who is watching might get inspired to do that.

So first, we aim to inform people about the field because it is still relatively new. A lot of people don’t really know about it. When I was going through my studies, my family and friends would ask “What is your major?” When I replied, “Biomedical engineering,” no one knew what I was talking about and I even struggled to explain it. We want to inform people about the field – that it exists, and that it is something that they can pursue. Second, we are trying to build a community for people in the field that could serve as a network, like “Oh I met this person through The BME Life!” We started a Facebook group that we hoped could help people find available positions. Then, third, without really meaning to do it, we have been consulting BME students. Students reach out and ask questions and we try to answer. That is definitely something that we would love to do more formally – to be able to offer consulting services. Recently, I was thinking, “Maybe we should have ‘The BME Life’ office hours open to the public for people to come and ask questions.”

So yeah, we started “The BME Life” in 2018. In the first year, it definitely grew pretty slow. It took quite a while to reach 100 subscribers on our YouTube channel, which is our main platform. Then, at some point, it started growing, we reached 500 subscribers, and it took off.

We would love for this to become something where we can create a company or program around in order to really support students and to collaborate with other programs in support of BME students and professionals. Right now, we are still playing it by ear, but it has been very fun, and I enjoy being part of this.

You have said “We” every time. Who else is part of “

The BME Life” team?

Actually, my boyfriend is a professional videographer! He mostly stays backstage. While I try to bring his face into the channel sometimes, he really likes working behind the scenes. So, I am the face and he records and edits everything. With time, I have really learned a lot from him, and can do some of the stuff on my own. But honestly, he is a bigger believe in The BME Life than I have ever been. He has always encouraged me to continue with the project and it has been so fun to do this with him.

As we near the end of our time together, I must know, what is next for you? Where do you see yourself going in the next 5 years, for example?

Honestly, it is hard to pinpoint something, especially now that I have entered this company. I am the one employee, and so I have to be pretty flexible while wearing different hats. I know for sure that I want to pursue a Master’s degree. I am still not sure about a PhD, but I am taking some time before grad school because I know there are many things I can do. Some programs combine engineering with business, and I am interested in that. There is also interesting work in the regulatory space and that excites me. Also, since I have been working at this startup, I honestly have had very little exposure to the basic sciences. While I was in my BME bachelors’ program, I was in a research lab for a year, but even in my classes I learned a lot of biology and chemistry. I have begun to really miss that. A part of me is interested in the business aspects of things and regulatory areas, but the other part of me misses science. I feel all over the place, but I am hoping to take a 2-3 year break before starting graduate school in order to make up my mind. I do think that I enjoy the medical device industry, but to really define by goals, I am not sure at the moment.

Sometimes, I even dream about starting my own company. It is an interest that I have because during my senior year at UTSA, we worked on a fetal monitor that was wearable. My dad is a gynecologist, so I always listen to him and what he does with his patients. I was personally very interested in this project because pregnant mothers have very limited options to monitor their baby’s health. One thing that we did for our project was creating this wearable monitor. While we weren’t able to pursue it beyond the course, I think it is something that I would be very interested in pursuing in the future. Like I said, I am playing it by ear, and seeing how things go.

Finally, with The BME Life, we definitely want it to grow and expand. Maybe, we can start adding some services, like consulting or advising. Maybe, we can start visiting schools for outreach programs. I saw online that one of the Whitaker Concluding Grants Initiatives includes high school outreach! I would love to learn more.

Thank you so much for your time on this Friday afternoon. Where are you off to right now?

I am actually parked outside of a restaurant called Chris Madrids. We’re about to eat. It’s honestly the best burger place in San Antonio. I highly recommend it!
