And what made you want to go abroad? What was that motivation to first go to the Netherlands and then go to the US? Can you tell me about that?

Curiosity? That was my reason. I had the possibility, it was something I had always wanted to do. Also in the summer, when I was younger, I used to travel a lot so I was already into it. When you have the chance to do it, you do it. And I wanted to see how the university life is different in countries and I wasn't sure I wanted to stay here, so it was like, you can go and see other stuff and decide, and for the moment I'm a visitor, so who knows, but I have time.

Maybe talk about the most surprising thing you found in the Netherlands and the US. Maybe there's a funny story, or wow, the folks are very different. Did you have a moment when you came back of reverse culture shock?

I don't know. I think America's very different from Europe. I mean, US and Italy are more different than the Netherlands and Italy. I found Dutch people really positive and happy, and I really like how they live. They are positive, open-minded, curious about other people. They all speak English, and you don't need to speak for sure their language that is like really really difficult. And the feeling is really positive. I really liked there.

New York as a city is amazing but, I don't know if I have to think for the rest of my life, I don't think I can see myself in New York. Big city, crazy city, it's, I don't know, too big, too much, too everything. From the personal point of view, I don't think this is a city I could live but for an experience, in New York you can find everything, so you're not in a single place. I was seriously surprised because a lot of Italians are there and not just tourists, also people who are working there. It happens to me twice in a day that I was in the center and someone on the phone was speaking in Italian and he was saying 'Oh, I just came out from my work' and he was speaking in Italian and it was like "okay, seriously?". So many Italians there.

Is there any cultural differences that you noticed between the US and -- maybe it's only specific to New York City, but maybe you can elaborate on the differences, you liked, you didn't like?

I think the biggest difference is that the fact that the health system is private is something that is crazy. Here it doesn't work like that and that's the reason. It's just different but it's something that for me is crazy, that if you have problem, you're not going to be treated -- and when you speak with Americans, they are really fine with that. It's normal for them but it's just the biggest difference I found. And the same is for universities. I found Americans, the one I met, that were almost all in the university, but they were super-motivated. I was working in medical school so it was medical program and they were super motivated. They knew already what they wanted to do. And the reason they were there was because they wanted to go in that place and it was like they knew already their future and I don't know if I'm not like them or it's just that here, we're more - we can change our mind, it's easier.

And one last question - what is something you are struggling with right now, that occupies a lot of your time?

I'm finding my PhD project and I would like to find something that is meaningful for people and not just because at the end I will have a PhD; but I think everyone wants to do something that is important and helpful to someone.
