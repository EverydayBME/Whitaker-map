Federico Barban

University of Genoa

8 April 2019 · 2 minute read

In this conversation

You will state your name and tell me a bit about your academic background.

I am Federico I study here in Genova. I'm from Genova, born and raised. I did my undergrad here and my master's as well. I studied biomedical engineering in undergrad and Bioengineering as master with neuro engineering [inaudible] specialization course.

What made you go into biomedical engineering?

Well, my family, both my parents are into medicine. They're a doctor and a nurse. And I was exposed to this kind of stuff since I was young, but I never wanted to become a medical doctor because you know, you see a lot of weird things. I don't like ... I'm not a people person, so I like ... the whole, you know, relationship with the patient wouldn't have done it for me, but still. And also I think I had it in me to become like kind of an engineer. I can remember he teachers in the primary school telling, "That kid will ... He's doing stuff. He will become an engineer, I think." And so it was. And actually at the very beginning I wanted to do prosthetics and because that looked like something that had a great impact, a great direct impact on people's life.

And what was the biggest shock for you in Kansas City?

The biggest shock ... Culture is very different. People are really friendly. Midwest friendly. As we were telling me before, like they do eye contact and they greet you and they say hello and that is kind of weird. But you get used to it and it's cool…I was just walking in, coming back from the university, I met this old native guy, and he said, "Good morning, young man." "What? Hello to you as well." Okay. All right. I got it. This is how you do it here. And then I kind of ... I got used to it in a way.

Tell me about what you're currently struggling with. What is like your biggest struggle right now?

I'm looking into like the finding meaning a lot in my life right now. I don't know if what I'm doing is the right way. If my choices were good enough. If my choices were right and whatever…Is the PhD the right choice, will it be useful to someone and am I just wasting, you know, these years of my life or whatever. Like, the finding meaning right now is a big issue for me. But on a practical point of view, I'm really busy with what I'm doing. So that takes up most of my time, a big chunk of time.
