Hannah Meredith

London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine (LSHTM)

How did you get interested in BME?

Sure. I guess my initial interest in biomedical engineering was the idea of using a quantitative approach to help improve health problems. And so I did my undergraduate degree in biomedical engineering, and then I followed it up with a PhD at Duke. Throughout that time I was always looking to see, what's the people factor? How can I have the most impact on people? I was really driven and motivated by the stories of people in the community who had these antibiotic resistant infections, and how that affected their quality of life. And then I started thinking, okay, there are a lot of people working on this from so many different angles. What's the best way to unite them and have broad sweeping impacts? I feel like working in public health, where you can work with policy makers, you can work with scientists, you can work with engineers and doctors. I feel like being able to tie everything into one, and then have a broader impact on the community. I felt like that was for me, it made sense as the way forward.

Can you tell us about what made you decide to pursue a PhD?

That's a tough question. Why does anyone really choose a PhD? There are a lot of different ways forward, but for me that seemed to be a way for me to choose a topic that I was interested in researching, and have a lot of independence, and gather more skillsets. But why did I choose biomedical engineering? I think it was the same reason that I got into the field in the first place, just using math and science together to try to improve medical situations.

What was the motivation, for you to do the Whitaker?

I really wanted to go abroad. So while I was in Grad School, I had this Aha moment that I was really interested in taking engineering abroad to the global health scene. And so, at Duke I actually got a global health Doctoral certificate, and during that time I got to spend a month in Sri Lanka, working on a fieldwork study with the group and I loved it. I love being outside. I love being out of the lab, but I also love being out of the country, and working with different cultures and seeing their perspective and the different health needs that are there. That is what really pushed me to go for the opportunity to work in another country, where they look at health problems differently. They have different perspectives, they have different resources. The people that they work with are different. And London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine is really well known for having a really big presence in the global health scene. The collaborators that they have, the people coming through and giving talks at the school, and the potential for doing field work was really great. That's why I selected there.

How'd you pick your lab that you joined at London school of Tropical, how'd you go about logistically figuring out the right fit for you?

There's actually another Whitaker fellow from Duke Biomedical Engineering, who had applied to the Whitaker's program the year before me, and she was at the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine. I was like, oh great London. I have family there. I've always wanted to go back and learn more about what it's like to live in the country. And also all the things that I said before about London School of Hygiene, Tropical Medicine, having a great global health presence. And then I was really interested in pushing forward the antibiotic resistance perspective that I had developed throughout Grad school. And so, I basically started googling, people working on antibiotic resistance and inter microbial resistance in general. That's how I found the program at LSHTM. I sent out a number of emails. One of them was really receptive to me coming, and immediately we started throwing around ideas for proposals to write up together. The rest is history.

So then, coming away from the stem side, can you talk about the cultural like experiences, or maybe some cultural adjustments you had following [inaudible]?

I can definitely, it's like a little guilty admission now. I definitely am obsessed with following the royal family news. It's like somehow Google has picked up on this, and I get all of the latest Meghan Markle, and all the royal baby stuff, which I was not into before, but gosh, the pub scenes create... I really miss the cultural side of things there, because museums were amazing. They were open late. There was just a lot of different opportunities for you to really get out and see different things in London. I think London in general was a really cool place to live. It was a huge city, so you had a wide range of different cultures present, you had a lot of food that you could try out. And then in terms of fun things that I did, I really enjoy walking. So I would go out into the countryside or I would take, I bought a bike when I was up there, and I would go biking through the countryside with some friends that I've made. It was just a nice way to get out and see different part of the country.
