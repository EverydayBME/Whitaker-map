How did you get interested in BME?

I guess initially I didn't really know what BME is about. So we had the college entrance exam in China, right. And my score wasn't that high. So even if I got into Tsinghua university, I can’t choose all of the majors I want. So there's like only a few that I can choose from. But I guess BME at the time sounds the most complicated and also interesting because I was always interested in medicine. Like, I wanted to be a doctor, but my mom said it was too stressful. And I also like engineering. So it's like a combination of those two. I didn't really have an idea or like what I'm getting into at the time.

So would you be willing to explain a little bit about like challenges in getting into an undergraduate university and you know, what the as kind of like in from a Chinese student perspective?

Yeah. It's purely based on the score you get in the college entrance exam. So like in the last year of high school, we basically study all day and night. So our school is like, I was in a small high school in Beijing and all students living in campus. So we can only get out on Sunday. And we also have study period after, after other classes until 11 PM I think. So there's always one teacher sitting there like studying or watching us study. It's basically our days from 6:00 AM to 11:00 PM all spent studying, do all kind of problems like mock exams. So that was really intense. And unlike American students I guess you have to do a lot of other activities like athletics or volunteer, but yeah, for us as long as we would get a good score, you can go to the best college.

how was it adjusting, from doing BME in Tsinghua to coming to the States and pursing graduate school?

Yeah, I feel I'm pretty fast on like adapting different environment. And so when I first arrived there was some other like to have students that was helping me finding place to live and have helped me do a lot of things. So that was pretty easy in terms of living. And for research I think I never did like more then three months research at a time. The only research before I did was because I was doing a little bit research in lab in Tsinghua but like mostly still taking classes at the undergraduate level.

Once I came here, I was helping two graduate students doing some programming stuff and also watching a lot of experiments. Yeah, I really learned a lot, which helped me for my PhD. And also I was pretty surprised by how they hold the lab meetings here. So people are like fighting each other and arguing with each other, which never happened in Tsinghua. I guess generally, Chinese students are less confrontational to each other or they kind of fear of fighting, especially there. So, but not quite the same here, but I feel I like it here. People always argue and respectfully disagree about specific questions and say things like, “you did this wrong, you should do this and do this”. Like they always have this kind of scientific conversation during lunchtime and I think it is intellectually stimulating.
