Background and career path

The happiest moment in my career path thus far was being accepted to the Biomedical Engineering PhD program at Purdue University directly after finishing my bachelor's degree at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. I am a first-generation college student so graduate school is such an amazing accomplishment - not only for me but for my entire family. As I wrap up the final months of my PhD program, I can definitively say that graduate school has changed my life. During graduate school, I became more independent, gained many new skills, and found a field of research that I am truly passionate about.

The international experience

An international experience was critical for me because my PhD thesis research combines global health and biomedical engineering. I have developed a low-cost, rapid paper-based test for detection of Vibrio cholerae in environmental water samples. Since cholera is not prevalent in the United States, it was important for me to travel to a cholera-endemic country to understand the current laboratory-based testing procedure and the challenges with this method. During my time in Haiti, I talked with field workers, lab technicians, doctors, and community health workers to better understand the difficulties in diagnosing cholera and monitoring the bacteria presence in the environment. These discussions guided my thesis work and helped me discover my interest in public health. I keep in contact with many of my colleagues in Haiti because they provide invaluable input in terms of device usability and feasibility.

Work and career reflections

I finished my Master of Public Health (MPH) degree just last week! Purdue University offers a dual-degree option within the Department of Public Health, so I started my MPH during my third year of my PhD program. I learned so much during my time in the MPH program and I believe it complements my knowledge of global health and my biomedical engineering skills. Additionally, I will be starting a postdoctoral position at Sandia National Laboratories in September working on point-of-care pathogen detection. I am excited for the next steps in my career and how I can integrate my expertise and passion for both public health and biomedical engineering.
