Erika Pliner

Neuroscience Research Australia (NeuRA)

2020 alumni reflection · 1 minute read

In this conversation

Background and career path

Dr. Kurt Beschorner has influenced my research career the most. He has been my outstanding advisor for 8 years, tailoring his mentoring to meet my career ambitions. My happiest moment along my training was my first publication.

The international experience

The Whitaker Fellowship facilitated the extension of my research on falls from workers in the occupational setting to older adults in the community. My knowledge of fall risk factors was expanded by quantifying individual factors (physical, cognitive and psychological) that contribute to ladder fall risk. This experience complemented my doctoral training in human movement biomechanics and prompted my interest in neuromechanical fall mechanisms. Thus, the Whitaker Fellowship had an effect on my desire to pursue a postdoctoral fellowship in neuromechanics.

Work and career reflections

I find the advances in non-invasive imaging during human locomotion most exciting. This technology can reveal the cognitive processes that facilitate biomechanical adaptations and responses. This knowledge is essential towards the design of effective interventions in falls prevention.
