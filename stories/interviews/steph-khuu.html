Stephanie (Steph) Khuu, PhD Candidate, Auckland Bioengineering Institute, University of Auckland, Auckland, New Zealand

May you please introduce yourself?

Hi, my name is Steph Khuu. I am a PhD candidate at the University of Auckland, specifically here in the Bioengineering Institute.

What is the focus of your PhD?

Broadly speaking, we are looking at cerebral palsy. [We study] the process of muscle regeneration and cerebral palsy using a combination of computational modeling and experimental work to validate that modeling.

What does your day-to-day look like?

I spend a lot of time at the computer coding for the main part of my project. For the other part, it is a lot of reading through literature. Hopefully, in a few months’ time, there will be a lot of lab work, which I am excited about because we will be working with muscle from the kids with cerebral palsy and typical developing kids to look at how the muscles regenerate and if they are regenerating properly.

Did you study bioengineering or biomedical engineering as an undergraduate?

No, I didn't. I have always had fascination with the engineering, but I actually studied physiology because I really love to understand how the human body works and what goes wrong when it doesn't work properly. I did my undergrad at the Faculty of Medical and Health Sciences, where focused on neurophysiology, or biomedical science applied to neuroscience. I was at The Center for Brain Research for my honors year, where we studied cells and how they can be used to test drugs for Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s. After that I took a break from studying, and I joined a group at the ABI that was modeling the brain for a project called Baby X.

I started working with Mark Sago, who is the head of the [Baby X project]. I did that for two years. They formed a spin-out company and they are based down in the city. I still work for them sometimes when I have time from my PhD, but it's not a lot of spare time. A couple of years ago I joined [the ABI]. I saw that Jeff had a project going. I took that project and now I'm trying to be a biomedical engineer. It is a lot of fun!

What does a biomedical engineer look like?

[Biomedical engineering] is disease focused, and I think the ways that you go about solving it is the engineering path. Biomedical engineers are the kinds of people who are cross-disciplinary in putting everything together to solve problems that humans have.

What excites you most about neuro prostheses?

I have this endless fascination with the human brain. I have a 3D printed miniature version of a human brain. That is what got me interested in engineering in the first place, since my first job was to recreate circuits from the human brain and put them in the, what we call, “digital human.” That was Baby X, and that is how I got started on that side.

Then, while doing all the research for that, I discovered that if you are creating a motor circuit, you should also want to know about neural control of movement. I discovered this whole field of limb prosthetics while just trolling through the literature. It would be really cool for people to be able to just control something naturally, especially if they need a prosthetic limb.

Where would you like to see your career go? Are you interested in academia, startups, or something else?

I think I have killed the startup desire, since I have been there, and done that. Also, I like a lot of organization, so the startup is not really where you get that. Academia would be nice, but I am under no illusion that it is easy to get where you want to be there. I could go into industry. It's always possible, I think. It just depends on the project itself. A career in academia would be really nice, and it would have to be something studying some kind of pathophysiology, like when something goes wrong in humans.

Do you find that you can manage a comfortable work-life balance?

Yes, that I do prioritize this a little bit. I prioritize mental health a lot, and I try to get a lot of exercise. I find that helps, and a good routine helps a lot. While I try not to work too much on the weekends or at nighttime, it does happen a lot. I figure that I want to be done with this in a good timeframe, so I need to put in an appropriate amount of work into that. It needs to be done, but, at the same time, you have to take care of yourself.

What advice would you give to someone who has emerging interest in biology, math and science?

I always say to myself now, "Say yes to everything and then you can pick and choose whenever you get there." I try and take advantage of all of the opportunities. Also, I try to ask a lot of questions. I know that I was too shy to ask a lot of questions when I was younger, but now I just ask questions all the time. It doesn't matter who it is or what kind of setting.

What is most exciting to you in your field right now?

This whole tissue collection thing is really exciting to us because it hasn't really been more than a couple of times before. To be able to obtain this kind of tissue from kids with cerebral palsy in New Zealand is quite a big deal for us. If we are able to get this experimental site underway, personally, I am really excited about it because I am trained in biomedical science and it'll add so much more to the PhD. I have been pouring a whole lot of effort into these applications just for this experimental validation because that would be most amazing.
