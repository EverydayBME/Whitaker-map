Say your name. Tell me a bit about your academic career so far.

I'm Cecilia De Vicariis. And I studied here, at University of Genoa, and I graduated in Biomedical Engineering last March. I received my education here in Genoa. Both, the bachelor degree and master degree. For my master’s, I spent six months in Marquette University, Milwaukee. While there, I worked in an international team while developing and improving some new skills, like languages, or my capability to deal with other people of different cultures.

What was the most challenging thing about that experience?  It can be technical, it can be personal.

I had a lot of technical challenges. I think that during my studies here in Genoa, we focused more on the theoretical part, while there, I had to build devices, or I had to use electronical components and develop some software. So, I had many challenges from the technological point of view during the thesis preparation. Another personal challenge was the 6:00 am morning Skype meeting each Thursday with my advisors. That was pretty hard to me.

What would say like the biggest cultural shock for you in the U.S. or in Marquette, Milwaukee?

For example, in the campus, I didn't know the fact that all the students have T-shirts and pants from the campus. They were all across campus... They're very proud of their college and to be part of that community.

And that was weird?

Yeah. To me, yes. A lot. In Italy I’ve never seen something like that and in that extent

Did you end up buying a T-shirt?

No, no. But, my professor bought me one at the end of the experience. As a present.
